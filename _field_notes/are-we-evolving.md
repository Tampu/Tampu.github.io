<p class="margin">not an essay with a conclusion. a question I'm still inside.</p>

<section class="part" markdown="1">
<div class="part-label"><span class="no">01</span><h2>The question</h2><span class="src">my own words</span></div>

We spend so much of our lives trying to understand our individual purpose. But what about the larger picture?

What is the purpose of the universe, if it has one? Is evolution simply a biological process, or can we think about it in terms of consciousness, knowledge and the development of intelligence?

Humanity has spent millions of years evolving into a species capable of understanding parts of the universe around it. And now we're building artificial intelligence that could one day surpass some of our own intellectual abilities.

<p class="big-q">Are we extending our evolution through technology, or creating something that could make our own intellectual development matter less?</p>
</section>

<section class="part" markdown="1">
<div class="part-label"><span class="no">02</span><h2>Questions I'm exploring</h2></div>

<ul class="wonder" markdown="0">
<li>Is evolution directional, or does it only appear that way to us?</li>
<li>What distinguishes intelligence from wisdom?</li>
<li>Can technological progress coexist with human intellectual growth?</li>
<li>What does it mean to evolve as a conscious species?</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section class="part" markdown="1">
<div class="part-label"><span class="no">03</span><h2>What I know so far</h2><span class="src">notes to self</span></div>

Not much, and some of it argues with the question.

**Biology doesn't promise a destination.** Biological evolution is change in heritable traits across generations. Nothing in it says life is heading somewhere, or that it must keep getting more intelligent. If evolution looks like a staircase, that might be because we're standing on the top step looking down.

**There are other kinds of change.** Culture evolves. Ideas evolve. Technology evolves, faster than anything with genes. Whether these are connected to biological evolution, or whether I'm projecting a sense of direction onto processes that have none, is part of what I want to find out.

**The soul is a different conversation.** Whether something like a soul grows or evolves belongs to philosophy, religion and metaphysics. Science hasn't said a soul evolves, and I don't want to borrow biology's authority for a question it doesn't answer.

**"AI will make us less intelligent" isn't settled.** It's a real worry. If we outsource memory, reasoning and problem-solving, we might lose some of them. AI could also let people learn and do far more than before. How it goes probably depends on how these systems are built and how we choose to use them, which is a strange thing to think about when you build them for a living.
</section>
